# Voiceover Script — The Woman Who Sold Her Husband's Secret

**Target runtime:** 5:00  
**Style:** Cinematic African moral-story documentary  
**Narrator:** Deep, calm, authoritative African storyteller; neutral international English  
**TTS:** Existing local Chatterbox only. No paid TTS.

---

## 00:00–00:35 — Hook

At midnight, Amara handed a folded piece of paper to a man waiting outside her home. He counted the money, smiled, and disappeared into the darkness. Amara thought she had just secured her future. But what she had actually sold was something far more valuable than a business secret. She had sold her husband's trust. And before the next sunset, the truth would come knocking at her door.

## 00:35–01:10 — Family

Amara and her husband, Chike, lived in a small village. They weren't wealthy. Their house was modest, and every day came with another financial challenge. But Chike was hardworking. He owned a tiny workshop where he prepared a product he hoped would one day support his family. Amara wanted a better life. She wanted a beautiful house, expensive clothes, and enough money to stop worrying about tomorrow. Chike wanted something simpler. He wanted to build their future slowly, honestly, and together.

## 01:10–01:50 — Discovery

One evening, after years of struggling, Chike discovered a method that made his product better than anything else in the market. At first, only a few people noticed. Then customers started arriving from neighbouring villages. Soon, his workshop was busy almost every day.

**Customer:** “Chike! What have you changed? This is better than before!”

**Chike:** “Nothing special. Just something I've been working on.”

But Amara was watching the money come in. And slowly, happiness became impatience.

## 01:50–02:30 — Offer

One afternoon, a wealthy trader named Bako approached Amara at the market.

**Bako:** “Your husband has something special.”

**Amara:** “What do you want?”

**Bako:** “The method he uses. I want to know how he makes that product.”

**Amara:** “Why are you asking me?”

**Bako:** “Because I believe you can help me. And I can pay you more money than you've ever seen.”

She knew it was wrong. But instead of walking away, she asked: How much?

## 02:30–03:10 — Betrayal

That night, Amara waited until Chike was asleep. She quietly opened the drawer where he kept his business notes. Inside was a notebook containing measurements, ingredients, and the method he had spent years developing. She took it. For a moment, she hesitated. Then she remembered Bako's money. At midnight, she met him outside the village.

**Bako:** “Did you bring it?”

**Amara:** “Yes.”

She handed him the notebook. He gave her the money.

**Bako:** “You have made the right decision.”

Amara smiled. She believed she had just changed her life. She was wrong.

## 03:10–03:55 — Discovery

The following morning, Chike received disturbing news. Another trader had started selling the same product. And according to the customers, it tasted almost exactly like his. Chike immediately became suspicious. When he returned home, he called his wife.

**Chike:** “Amara, did you enter my workshop last night?”

**Amara:** “No.”

Chike looked at her.

**Chike:** “Then where is my notebook?”

Amara froze. Before she could answer, someone knocked. It was Bako.

## 03:55–04:35 — Twist

Chike looked at Bako, then at his wife.

**Chike:** “You bought my secret from her, didn't you?”

**Bako:** “I paid for your formula. Now I can make the same product.”

But Chike did something neither of them expected. He walked to a cupboard and brought out another notebook.

**Chike:** “The notebook she gave you wasn't my real formula.”

**Amara:** “What do you mean?”

Chike explained that the stolen notebook contained incomplete measurements. He had kept the complete method somewhere else because he knew someone might eventually try to steal his work. Bako had paid a fortune for information that could never reproduce the original product.

## 04:35–05:00 — Consequence and Moral

Bako left furious. Amara began to cry.

**Amara:** “Chike, can you ever forgive me?”

**Chike:** “I can forgive you. But trust is something you will have to earn again.”

Weeks later, Amara began helping him at the workshop. She had finally understood that wanting a better life was not wrong. But betraying the person building that life beside you was never the answer. Remember this: Greed can make you sell what money can never buy back — trust.
